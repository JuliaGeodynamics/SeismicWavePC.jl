Models

The model

A model is a GMG CartData (coordinates in km, z up; constant spacing in x and y, see below for z) with the fields

  • rho [kg/m³] (or g/cm³ with density_unit = :g_cm3, as in OpenSWPC),
  • vp, vs [km/s],
  • optionally Qp, Qs (without them the model is elastic). Q is constant between fq_min and fq_max, and the velocities apply at fq_ref (keywords of simulate; defaults 0.05, 5 and 1 Hz).

Cells with vp = vs = 0 are air: the free surface and topography follow from them. Cells with vs = 0 and vp > 0 are water (oceans, lakes). A CartData of size (nx, 1, nz) is a 2D model (P-SV by default, wave = :sh for SH waves). Any GMG tool can be used to build the model: layers, volcanoes, spheres, slabs, geographic data and projections.

Finer cells near the surface

The spacing in z may vary: give the CartData the depths you want, for example fine cells in slow near-surface layers (sediments, volcanic deposits) and coarser ones at depth. The solver then works on a grid stretched in depth (the depth derivatives are taken along the cell index and divided by the local spacing, with 4th-order consistent factors), so that the waves see the same physics with far fewer cells. The spacing must change smoothly, by at most 25 % from cell to cell (a few percent is best). The spacing in x and y stays constant.

depths, h = [0.0025], 0.005                         # cell centres [km]: 5 m cells down to 250 m,
while depths[end] < 3.0                              # then 3 % larger per cell up to 40 m
    hn = depths[end] < 0.25 ? 0.005 : min(1.03h, 0.04)
    push!(depths, depths[end] + (h + hn) / 2)
    h = hn
end
z = [reverse(-depths); 0.0025; 0.0075]              # z up, with 2 cells of air on top
X, Y, Z = xyz_grid(range(0, 4, step=0.005), 0, z)

Everything else works as on a uniform grid: sources and stations (also between grid points), the PML (its profile follows the actual depths), MPI, GPUs, and the exact adjoint gradients. The time step is limited by the smallest cells, as before: dt < 6/7 / (vp √(1/dx² + 1/dz²)) layer by layer, so fine cells in slow rock cost little extra. Plane waves need a uniform grid.

Example: a 250 m thick layer with vs = 0.4 km/s over faster rock (4 Hz Ricker, P-SV, stations 10 m deep), compared with a uniform 5 m grid:

Geographic coordinates

Models built from geographic data with a GMG ProjectionPoint keep their projection: sources and stations can be given in longitude and latitude with the same ProjectionPoint (Sources and stations).

Models from thermomechanical and petrological models

The temperature of a MagmaThermoKinematics.jl model, or any other temperature field, can be turned into density, seismic velocities and melt fraction with MAGEMin phase diagrams, including the reduction of the velocities by partial melt and a low Q in partially molten rock. See the Unzen examples in Examples.

Random media

xi = random_medium(x, y, z; spectrum=:vonkarman, a=0.5, epsilon=0.05, kappa=0.3)
model = perturb(model, xi)

makes fluctuations with a Gaussian, exponential or von Kármán spectrum (anisotropic with a=(ax, ay, az)) and applies them as OpenSWPC does (vp, vs × (1 + ξ), ρ × (1 + 0.8 ξ) in solid rock). read_random_medium(file) reads OpenSWPC's random media.

Layered models and OpenSWPC files

read_lhm, read_lgm and read_grd build models from layered descriptions (with OpenSWPC's options vcut, munk and earth_flattening), and read_netcdf reads OpenSWPC's netCDF velocity models; see Coming from OpenSWPC. flatten_earth(model) applies the Earth-flattening transformation to any model.
